# The Maker Lane — questions about the game itself

Until now the graph had two kinds of questions: how a performer
plays (Show and Coach lanes) and how a coach steers (nudge and role
questions). Casey added a third kind on 2026-10-14: questions a
*maker* asks about a game while it is being played.

- What is this system trying to make the player feel, and what is
  the cheapest trick it uses to do it?
- Where is the fun actually manufactured — a number, a timer, a
  scarcity, a physics edge?
- What would break if this rule changed, and who would notice
  first, the player or the designer?
- Is this moment designed, emergent, or a bug the makers kept?

Why the avatar needs maker questions, not just performer ones:

1. **Reading a new game fast.** A performer avatar that can ask
   maker questions of an unfamiliar game finds its staircases,
   its stake structure and its breakable systems in minutes
   instead of hours — the same reading SplatterCat does as a
   buyer's guide, generalised.
2. **Coaching upward.** The kid coach eventually stops nudging
   the avatar and starts asking why the game fights back.
   Maker questions are that curriculum's second year.
3. **Our own games.** Coach Player will be played inside games
   Casey builds. An avatar that understands the designer's
   intent can perform the design instead of merely surviving it.

Maker questions live in the same database with mode "maker" where
they are about the game, or the mode they serve. They shade the
same way: a percentage, evidence, and a named pole at each end.
Their evidence is maker and analyst transcripts (devlogs, design
essays, tutorials), sampled from the Maker Lane roster under the
same paraphrase and no-clone rules as everyone else.
