# Show Mode Questions — Notes

40 spectrum questions in `show-mode.jsonl` (show-001 … show-040),
all status `hypothesis`. `winner_lean` is a provisional guess at where
this lane's successful creators cluster; `spread` is how far apart
they sit from each other. Wide spread + a lean away from 50 is what
makes a question worth running a Jev on.

## The 5 most discriminating

1. **show-002 — Engineer the spectacle vs discover it.**
   Splits the lane cleanly: Neotastic / GrayStillPlays / Jesse Gillett
   build the disaster on purpose; UnspeakablePlays and OMB mostly
   walk into theirs. The answer decides whether our avatar is a
   designer who stages experiments or a witness who reacts — two
   different pilots, two different voices.

2. **show-005 — Commentator outside the game vs character inside it.**
   OMB's roleplay is the widest behavioural outlier in the whole
   roster. High on this axis, the avatar is a scene partner
   improvising with others; low, it's a narrator. Almost every other
   setting (lines, memory, humour) changes depending on this one.

3. **show-004 — Failure as the payoff vs failure as a setback.**
   The soul of the physics-sandbox lane: is the sinking the win or
   the mistake? It governs reset behaviour, apology language, and
   what the avatar celebrates — and the winners genuinely disagree.

4. **show-029 — Gleeful villain vs heroic helper.**
   Gray's villain-narrator is a magnet for this age group, OMB and
   Unspeakable lean helpful, and our product must cap this axis
   knowingly (humour never cruel at a person). A question we need
   measured precisely *because* we intend to overrule the winners
   at one end of it.

5. **show-022 — Narrating the physics vs narrating only the outcome.**
   The educational axis, and the one with the most honest
   uncertainty (lean 45, wide spread). For tech-curious
   middle-schoolers, cause-and-effect talk ("it's tipping because
   the cargo shifted") may be the lane's hidden value — or may be
   something only the small channels do while the giants skip it.

## A question I could not phrase as a spectrum

**What does the creator *never* do?**
The nevers — never fakes a result, never aims a joke at a real kid
in the audience, never leaves a young viewer not knowing whether
something worked — are a categorical list, not a dial. Low/high
phrasing ("how never do they…") is meaningless, and a 0–100 score
would hide exactly the information that matters: *which* lines are
never crossed. This one belongs in the codex as an explicit
boundary list per creator, to become the avatar's guardrails.
