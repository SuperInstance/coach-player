# Nudging & Roleplay Questions — Notes

Lane: the learning loop of nudging (nudge-001…020) and roleplay /
in-character play (role-001…020), the latter with OMB Gaming's
collaborative Roblox roleplay as the roster reference.

## The 5 most discriminating questions

1. **nudge-003 — free-text orders vs preset buttons/sliders.** This is
   the curriculum question. Free text is where a kid actually learns
   directing and prompting; presets are where reliability lives. Where
   a design sits here decides whether Coach Player teaches a transferable
   skill or just operates a toy. Wide spread, no settled answer.

2. **nudge-012 — can the owner rewrite a proposed personality change,
   or only accept/reject it?** Accept/reject makes the kid a judge;
   rewriting makes the kid an author. It is the sharpest line between
   "rating an agent's growth" and "shaping a character who is yours,"
   and it changes what the owner rite feels like to perform.

3. **nudge-018 — should the avatar push back on a nudge that fights
   its ruled sheet?** Instant compliance maximises felt control;
   a confirmation prompt makes every past ruling weigh something.
   This question separates agents that feel like tools from agents
   that feel like characters — the exact thing the product is selling.

4. **role-010 — witnessed-only knowledge vs omniscient narration.**
   An in-role character that only knows what it saw can be surprised,
   wrong, and honest; an omniscient one is secretly reading the pilot's
   game state, which breaks both the fiction and the no-cheating-state
   rule the pilot layer runs on. Strong provisional lean (78) toward
   witnessed-only, and it is architectural, not cosmetic.

5. **role-014 — can the coach freeze a roleplay scene at any moment?**
   Interruptibility is a guardrail in Coach mode, but a scene that can
   always be frozen trains the coach to direct instead of trust.
   Where this lands decides whether roleplay is a performance the kid
   watches with faith in the avatar, or another control surface.

## Tensions found: kid agency vs the guardrails

- **Pushback vs control.** nudge-018 and role-009 (hold ruled traits at
  a cost) both spend the kid's moment-to-moment control to buy character
  consistency. Too much pushback and the coach feels managed by their
  own agent; too little and their rulings mean nothing.
- **Memory editing vs memory honesty.** nudge-013 leans toward fully
  inspectable, editable memory — but the sheet's guardrail says the
  avatar may never claim a memory it does not have. If a kid can edit
  memory, kid-authored entries need visible provenance, or the honesty
  rail is quietly broken by the feature meant to teach it.
- **Rail bounces that teach vs rails that tempt.** nudge-015's
  "explain and offer the nearest allowed nudge" keeps a kid shaping —
  but every explained boundary is also a map for probing loopholes.
  The bounce has to teach the *reason* without turning the rail into
  a negotiation.
- **Peer examples vs authorship.** nudge-020: seeing how others nudged
  is the fastest teacher and the fastest way to make every kid's agent
  converge on the popular build. For a middle-school audience this is
  also a privacy/modelling question, not just a design one.
- **Persistence vs forgotten nudges.** nudge-005: carry-over nudges
  respect the kid's intent, but a forgotten temporary mood becoming
  permanent personality is exactly the failure the nudge/rule
  distinction exists to prevent.
- **In-scene conflict vs family-friendly tone.** role-012: roleplay
  needs disagreement to have stories, and OMB's lane proves conflict
  can stay friendly — but the avatar, not the kids, has to carry the
  work of keeping in-character conflict from reading as real conflict.
