# Maker codex — aggregate v0 (first wave, six makers)

Sampled 2026-10-14 via the browser route (TranscriptAPI plan
exhausted; panel succeeded 3 of 6, caption fallback covered
the rest, methods declared per entry). 52 paraphrased entries
in maker-v0.jsonl: Juniper Dev, CodyCantEatThis, Goodgis,
Jack Sather, Code Monkey, Indie Game Clinic — one video each,
hypothesis grade throughout. Nothing here is a trait yet; the
house rule stands (a behaviour is a trait only if it survives
a second video and a format change).

## The wave's law, with its boundary

**Behaviour gates the start; feel gates the finish; data
carries the middle.**

- Whether to build at all is judged by strangers' observed
  behaviour, not by the maker's taste (Code Monkey: clips and
  wishlists from a mockup of a game that does not exist).
- Craft in progress is judged by feel (Juniper communicates
  state through atmosphere; Cody condemns purposelessness,
  not roughness; Goodgis iterates until the world feels
  alive; Sather's evidence standard is his own felt
  response, including perceptual after-effects).
- Between those poles, systems work is judged by data and
  simulation (Indie Game Clinic: master variables in player
  time, data-driven tuning over micro-tweaks, playtests run
  at randomised progression states).
The apparent contradiction (feel vs evidence) dissolves by
stage: each court of appeal rules a different phase.

## Answer templates (reusable shapes, for mix-and-match)

Named per Casey's directive that digestion yield templates of
answers, not only evidence. Each is stated as the avatar
would use it while reading or playing an unfamiliar game.

- **T1 — Find the constraint.** Ask what limits the player;
  read every other system as downstream of that one pressure.
  (Sather's reading method; Cody's one-mechanic anchor.)
- **T2 — Two gates.** Fun gate and want gate are separate
  questions with separate cheap tests; never let one artefact
  answer both. (Code Monkey.)
- **T3 — The feel pass.** Iterate until it feels alive;
  communicate state through the world (light, layout) before
  reaching for numbers and HUD. (Goodgis, Juniper.)
- **T4 — Systems over tweaks.** When something rots or needs
  tuning, change the data model or build the small tool;
  refuse per-item hand edits. (Goodgis's cutscene engine;
  Indie Game Clinic's master variables.)
- **T5 — Honest surface.** No fabricated shots, no staged
  evidence; credibility is the asset and hype is earned.
  (Goodgis; Cody judging by intent; Sather disclosing the
  sponsorship up front.)
- **T6 — Design before implementing.** A build without a
  prior design reads as a testing scene, not a game.
  (Cody's stated principle; Indie Game Clinic's progression
  architecture precedes content.)
- **T7 — Scope to learn.** Small, parallel, disposable builds
  sized to the question — or to the team you actually have.
  (Cody's twin builds; Code Monkey's prototypes; Juniper
  recruiting to scope.)
- **T8 — Cheap failure invites trying.** Progression as
  information architecture: hide complexity until ready, keep
  failure cheap so curiosity wins. (Indie Game Clinic;
  Juniper's post-mortem is the negative example — siloed,
  untested mechanics are what happens without it.)

## Tensions kept open

- **Art early**: morale infrastructure for a team (Juniper)
  vs polish-before-proof as a trap (Code Monkey, Cody).
  Resolution so far: art early serves the BUILDERS; polish
  as a product claim before validation is the trap. Stage
  again, not contradiction.
- **Playtesting** is named most strongly by its absence
  (Juniper's post-mortem). Nobody in wave 1 runs formal
  playtests of systems; feedback is trusted circles
  (Goodgis), state simulation (Indie Game Clinic), or the
  market itself (Code Monkey). maker-005/006 evidence should
  record the stance as: playtesting is real but informal and
  stage-specific.
