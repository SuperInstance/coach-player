## What surprised me

OMB Gaming:
- There is no intro. The video starts mid-arrest, already in
  character, and the premise only arrives minutes later, smuggled
  inside a scouting briefing. Immersion is the cold open.
- Most of the runtime is planning, travel and logistics. The raid
  itself is roughly the last two minutes of thirteen and a half,
  and the video works anyway, because the plan is the drama and
  the raid is only its exam.
- He is less a player in the scene than its director inside it:
  allied players get cast, blocked, timed and debriefed, all
  without him ever stepping out to run the production.

Jesse Gillett:
- This sampled video has no co-op partner at all. He flies,
  narrates and witnesses alone, so the narrate-versus-witness
  question the pair was chosen for plays out inside one person:
  pilot during setup, hands-off witness at impact, forensic
  investigator afterwards. Any claim about his co-op banter
  needs a different video; this one cannot carry it.
- His register barely escalates. Aircraft break up at extreme
  speed and the voice stays near conversational, with short
  amazement beats. The spectacle does the exciting; he does
  the witnessing, which inverts the usual reactor pattern.
- The celebration is the smallest part of every success. One
  disbelief line, then the real reward: a long, pleased,
  item-by-item reading of the wreckage, ending in an actual
  reconstruction of the aircraft. Failure and survival are
  both paid out in forensics, not in cheering.
